1. About this notice

This notice explains how Dr [Firstname] [Lastname] ("we", "our", "the Practice") handles personal data, and how cookies and similar technologies are used on this website (www.amoncology.com).

It is written in two parts, because the two situations are very different:

  • Part A applies to anyone who simply visits this website. Almost no personal data is involved.
  • Part B applies if you become a patient. This involves detailed health information and is governed by additional legal and professional duties.

We handle personal data in accordance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018 (both as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025), and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR).

Part A — Visitors to this website

This part applies to everyone who reads this site. You do not need to be a patient.

A1. We do not collect data through this website

There are no forms on this site. There is no contact form, no newsletter sign-up, no patient portal, no online booking and no payment facility. Nothing you type is submitted to us, because there is nowhere to type it.

The only ways to reach us are the published email address and telephone number. Those are contacts you choose to initiate, and are covered by section 3 below.

A2. How the site is built and hosted

This is a static website. The pages, images and fonts are stored as fixed files and sent to your browser unchanged; there is no database and no server-side processing of visitor information.

The site's source files are held in a private code repository on GitHub, and the site is published and delivered to visitors by Cloudflare. [Amend if you use Cloudflare in front of GitHub Pages: in that configuration GitHub also acts as the origin server and receives page requests.]

All fonts and images are served from our own domain. This site makes no requests to third-party font services, image libraries, advertising networks, social media platforms or analytics providers.

Technical logs

As with any website, Cloudflare automatically records limited technical information in order to deliver pages and protect the site from attack — typically the IP address, the page requested, the time of the request, and basic browser and device information.

  • Purpose: delivering the website, maintaining its security and availability, and preventing malicious traffic.
  • Lawful basis: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) UK GDPR) — our interest in operating a secure, functioning website, which we consider does not override your rights given how little information is involved.
  • Retention: these logs are held by Cloudflare for a short period under its standard retention settings and are not copied, exported or combined with any other information by us.
  • Use: we do not use this information to identify individual visitors, build profiles, or track you across other websites.

International transfers

Cloudflare and GitHub are US-headquartered companies operating global infrastructure, so the limited technical data described above may be processed outside the United Kingdom. Where that happens, transfers are covered by the safeguards those providers maintain for UK and EU customers — the International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, or the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. No clinical or health information is transferred, because none is submitted through this website.

A3. Cookies and similar technologies

PECR governs anything that stores information on, or reads information from, your device — cookies, local storage, tracking pixels and similar technologies.

This website sets no analytics, advertising, marketing or tracking technologies of any kind. No consent banner is displayed because nothing is used that requires your consent.

Cloudflare may set a small number of strictly necessary cookies to keep the site secure and available — for example, to distinguish genuine visitors from automated traffic. These fall within the "strictly necessary" exception in PECR and cannot be switched off without affecting delivery of the site itself.

Part B — Patients and clinical care

This part applies only if you are referred to, or treated by, the Practice. None of it is triggered by visiting this website.

B1. What we hold and where it comes from

To provide clinical care we hold:

  • Personal identifiers — name, date of birth, NHS number.
  • Contact details — address, telephone, email.
  • Clinical information — medical history, symptoms, examination findings, diagnostic imaging (CT, MRI, PET), pathology and laboratory results, treatment plans and correspondence.
  • Where applicable, private medical insurance and billing details.

Some of this comes directly from you. A significant proportion reaches us from other sources, including your GP, referring clinicians, hospital records systems, diagnostic providers, multidisciplinary team meetings, and your insurer where relevant. We may also receive information from a relative or carer acting on your behalf.

B2. Lawful bases for clinical processing

Health information is "special category" data and needs a basis under both Article 6 and Article 9 of the UK GDPR:

  • Article 6(1)(b) — performance of a contract, where we have agreed to provide private care.
  • Article 6(1)(c) — legal obligation, for example clinical record-keeping and financial or tax reporting.
  • Article 6(1)(f) — legitimate interests, for administration of the Practice and defence of legal claims.
  • Article 9(2)(h) — medical diagnosis and the provision of health care and treatment, together with the condition at Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2 of the Data Protection Act 2018. This processing is carried out by, or under the responsibility of, a professional subject to a duty of confidentiality under Article 9(3).
  • Article 9(2)(f) — establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims, if that becomes necessary.

Separately from data protection law, we owe you a professional duty of confidentiality, and we follow General Medical Council guidance on confidentiality and disclosure.

B3. Who your information is shared with

To provide safe and effective care, we may share relevant information with:

  • Your GP and any referring or treating consultants.
  • Hospitals and diagnostic providers delivering scans, tests, radiotherapy or drug treatment.
  • Multidisciplinary teams, for peer review of diagnosis and treatment planning.
  • Your private medical insurer, for authorisation and billing, where you are insured.
  • National cancer registries and statutory bodies, where disclosure is required or permitted by law.
  • Regulators, auditors and professional indemnity organisations, where required by law or necessary to respond to a complaint or claim.

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for marketing.

Records held by the hospital

Where you are seen at a hospital or clinic, that provider keeps its own record of your care and is a separate data controller in its own right, with its own privacy notice, retention arrangements and complaints procedure. This notice covers the records we hold as your treating consultant. If you want to see or query the hospital's record, or raise a concern about the care delivered at that site, you will usually need to approach the provider directly, and we can tell you who to contact.

B4. Retention and automated decision-making

We retain clinical records with regard to the Records Management Code of Practice for Health and Social Care, which we follow as best practice. Adult health records are generally kept for a minimum of 8 years after the conclusion of treatment. Records may be kept longer where there is a continuing clinical need, an ongoing or anticipated legal claim, or a specific legal requirement. Records relating to anyone under 18 are retained until at least their 25th birthday. Financial records are kept for 6 years in line with HMRC requirements.

We do not make decisions about your care by automated means, and we do not carry out profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

3. Communicating with us

If you email or telephone us, we will hold that correspondence in order to respond to it and, where you are a patient, as part of your record.

Ordinary email is not a secure medium. Messages can be intercepted, delayed, misdirected or fail to arrive. We would therefore ask you not to send detailed clinical information, scans or reports by ordinary email. Where sensitive material needs to be transferred, we will arrange a secure route — NHS-to-NHS mail, encrypted transfer, or post. If you choose to email us anyway, we will treat what you send confidentially, but we cannot control the security of the message in transit.

Email and this website are not monitored for urgent or emergency problems. Messages may not be read for some time, and no clinical advice is given by email outside an established consultation.

If you are undergoing treatment and become unwell — for example with a fever, chest pain, or uncontrolled symptoms — contact the 24-hour Acute Oncology Service number you were given at the start of treatment.

In a life-threatening emergency, dial 999 or attend your nearest A&E department.

4. Your rights

Under the UK GDPR you have the right to:

  • Be informed about how your data is used — the purpose of this notice.
  • Access your records, by making a subject access request. We will normally respond within one month.
  • Rectification of inaccurate personal data. In clinical records, a factual error will be corrected; where a professional opinion is disputed, the record is annotated with your comments rather than altered, so that the clinical history remains intelligible.
  • Erasure in certain circumstances. This right is limited for clinical records, which we are required to retain (Article 17(3)).
  • Restriction of processing in certain circumstances.
  • Data portability, which applies to data you provided that we process by automated means under contract or consent.
  • Object to processing carried out on the basis of legitimate interests. We will stop unless we have compelling grounds to continue, though this may affect our ability to provide safe care.
  • Withdraw consent at any time, where we have relied on consent. Most clinical processing does not rely on consent, so withdrawing it will not usually stop us holding your records.

These rights are free to exercise. Use the contact details in section 2, and we may ask you to verify your identity before we release information.

5. Complaints

About how we have handled your data

If you believe we have not handled your personal data properly, please tell us first, using the contact details in section 2. Since 19 June 2026, section 164A of the Data Protection Act 2018 gives you a statutory right to complain directly to us, and requires us to have a process for dealing with it. We will:

  • acknowledge your complaint within 30 days of receiving it;
  • make appropriate enquiries and keep you updated;
  • tell you the outcome without undue delay, and explain how to escalate it.

You can complain in whatever way suits you — email, letter or telephone — and you do not need to use any particular form of words.

If you remain dissatisfied, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or on 0303 123 1113. You can approach the ICO at any time, but raising it with us first is usually quicker.

About your clinical care

Complaints about the care itself are handled separately from data protection complaints, and the route depends on where you were seen.

For NHS care, contact the hospital's Patient Advice and Liaison Service (PALS). For care at a private hospital or clinic, please raise the matter with us or with the hospital directly; because care there is delivered under that provider's governance arrangements, its own complaints procedure will apply, and unresolved complaints may be escalated to the independent adjudication scheme the provider subscribes to. We will tell you who to contact and will co-operate fully with any investigation.
