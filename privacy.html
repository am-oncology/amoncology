1. Introduction and scope

This policy explains how Dr Asad Mahmood ("we", "our", "the Practice") collects, uses, shares and protects personal information, and how this website uses cookies and similar technologies. It applies to www.amoncology.com and to the private medical practice of Dr Asad Mahmood.

We are committed to handling your information lawfully, fairly and transparently, in accordance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), the Data Protection Act 2018, the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR), and the confidentiality standards set by the General Medical Council and the common law duty of confidence.

Please note: this website is an information resource only. It does not host a patient portal, booking system or clinical records system, and no medical records are stored on it. Clinical information is held separately, within the secure systems described in this policy.

Where care is delivered at a private hospital or clinic, that provider is a separate data controller for the records it holds, and its privacy notice will apply alongside this one.

3. Information we collect

A. Website visitors

This website is a static, informational site. We do not operate contact forms, accounts or logins, and we do not use marketing cookies, advertising pixels or cross-site tracking.

Our hosting provider automatically processes limited technical information in order to serve the site securely and reliably. This includes your IP address, the date and time of your request, the pages requested, and basic browser and device information. This data is used for security, abuse prevention and performance, and is not used to build a profile of you or to identify you personally.

B. People who contact us

If you email or telephone the Practice, we will hold your contact details and the content of your message so that we can respond and, where relevant, keep a record of the enquiry. Enquiries that do not lead to a clinical relationship are retained only for as long as needed to deal with the matter and any follow-up.

C. Patients

When you become a patient of the private practice, we collect and hold information necessary to provide safe clinical care, including:

  • Personal identifiers such as your name, date of birth, address and NHS number.
  • Contact details, including telephone number and email address, and your preferences for how we contact you.
  • Your GP and referrer details.
  • Medical history, examination findings, diagnostic and pathology reports, imaging (such as CT, MRI and PET), radiotherapy planning data and treatment records.
  • Correspondence about your care, including letters to and from other clinicians.
  • Insurance policy and authorisation details, or billing and payment information where you are funding your own care.

Most health information is classed as "special category data" under UK GDPR and is given additional protection. Some information may also relate to criminal convictions or other sensitive matters if disclosed to us as part of your medical history; this is handled with the same care.

5. Cookies and similar technologies

What cookies are

Cookies are small text files placed on your device when you visit a website. They allow a site to recognise your device, remember your preferences, and operate securely. Similar technologies include local storage, session storage and tracking pixels. Under PECR, we may only place cookies that are not strictly necessary for the service you have requested if you have given your consent.

Cookies used on this website

This website does not set any of its own cookies. It has no login, no shopping basket and no personalisation features, so there is nothing for a first-party cookie to remember.

Our content delivery and security provider, Cloudflare, may set the following strictly necessary cookies to protect the site from automated abuse and malicious traffic. These are exempt from the consent requirement because they are essential to delivering the service securely.

We use Cloudflare Web Analytics to understand how many people visit the site and which pages are most useful. Because no information is stored on or retrieved from your device, your consent is not required.

We do not use Google Analytics, advertising networks, social media tracking pixels, or any form of behavioural profiling.

7. Managing your cookie preferences

Because this site sets no non-essential cookies, there is no cookie banner or preference centre to configure. You remain in full control of cookies through your browser settings, where you can view, delete and block cookies for any website.

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies
  • Safari: Settings → Safari → Privacy & Security
  • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions
  • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security

Blocking the strictly necessary cookies listed above may mean you are repeatedly presented with security checks, but the site will otherwise function normally. General guidance on cookies is available from the ICO at ico.org.uk.

We honour "Global Privacy Control" and "Do Not Track" signals by default, in the sense that we carry out no tracking of any visitor regardless of the signal sent.

11. Security and email communication

We take appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect your information, including encryption in transit, access controls, secure storage of clinical records, and confidentiality obligations on everyone working with the Practice. This website is served over HTTPS.

Important — email is not secure. Standard internet email is not an encrypted medium and messages can, in principle, be intercepted or misdirected. Please do not send clinical details, medical records, scan images or other sensitive information by ordinary email. If you contact us by email, please keep the message to a brief request and we will arrange a secure route for anything confidential.

Referrers should use NHS.net-to-NHS.net mail or another encrypted transfer method for reports and imaging. If you choose to send sensitive information by unencrypted email despite this warning, you accept the associated risk.

If a personal data breach occurs that is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will report it to the ICO within 72 hours and, where the risk is high, inform you directly without undue delay.

12. Your rights

Under UK GDPR you have the following rights, which you can exercise free of charge in most circumstances:

  • Access — to obtain a copy of the personal information we hold about you (a Subject Access Request).
  • Rectification — to have inaccurate information corrected. In medical records, a factual error will be corrected; a clinical opinion you disagree with cannot be deleted, but your comments will be added to the record.
  • Erasure — to ask for information to be deleted. This right is limited where we have a legal or professional obligation to retain medical records.
  • Restriction — to ask us to limit how we use your information while a concern is resolved.
  • Portability — to receive certain information in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format.
  • Objection — to object to processing carried out on the basis of legitimate interests.
  • Withdrawal of consent — where we rely on consent, you may withdraw it at any time, without affecting processing already carried out.

We will respond to a request within one calendar month. This may be extended by up to two further months for complex requests, in which case we will tell you and explain why. We may ask you to verify your identity before disclosing information.

Please note that objecting to or restricting the processing of your clinical information may affect our ability to provide safe care, and we will discuss the implications with you.
