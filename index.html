Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy uses precisely targeted radiation, usually high-energy x-rays, to treat cancer and certain benign conditions. It can be delivered as external radiotherapy, aimed at the tumour from outside the body, or as internal radiotherapy (brachytherapy), where the radiation source is placed close to the tumour itself.

Learn more →

Systemic Anti-Cancer Therapy (SACT)

Drug treatments travel through the bloodstream to reach cancer wherever it has spread. It includes chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted agents and hormone therapy. The choice depends on the tumour type and its molecular profile, and your general health and treatment goals. Drugs are sometimes given alongside radiotherapy as chemoradiotherapy.

Research & Publications

Research drives every improvement in cancer care, from earlier detection through to treatments that are more effective and better tolerated. Clinical trials test new drugs, radiotherapy techniques and treatment combinations against current standards, while pre-clinical scientific studies explore how tumours grow, spread and develop resistance. Findings from both feed back into everyday practice.
